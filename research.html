Research Projects

🔹 ChainSentinel: AI-Powered Multi-Agent Framework for Smart Contract Security

Objective: Design a domain-specific, multi-agent framework that integrates large language models (LLMs) with static and dynamic analysis tools (e.g., Slither, Mythril) to enhance security auditing, reduce false positives, and improve explainability in smart contracts.

  • Implements Auditor–Critic–Validator agent collaboration for iterative and explainable vulnerability detection.
  • Uses annotated datasets from SmartBugs Curated and NotSoSmartContracts for fine-tuning and benchmarking.
  • Combines formal verification with natural-language reasoning to generate human-readable security reports.

🔹 Benchmarking Smart Contract Auditing Tools — ESEM 2026

Published at the 20th International Symposium on Empirical Software Engineering and Measurement (ESEM 2026). This study evaluates seven static-analysis and LLM-based smart contract auditing tools on 143 contracts from SmartBugs Curated and 173 real-world, audit-derived contracts from FORGE.

Using a tool-agnostic evaluation pipeline, we compared vulnerability detection accuracy, top-K detection, execution robustness, and performance across datasets. Static analyzers often over-report vulnerability categories and show stability limitations on real-world contracts; LLM-based tools provide broad detection coverage but can also over-predict. The study highlights the need for precise and reliable auditing rather than broad detection alone.

  • Authors: Shawal Khalid, Joaquin Tuckett, and Chris Brown.
  • Publication: ESEM 2026, LIPIcs Vol. 394, Article 45, pp. 45:1–45:21.
  • Paper: 10.4230/LIPIcs.ESEM.2026.45 · Benchmarking code

🔹 Capabilities of Generative AI for Smart Contract Development

Empirical multi-phase study investigating how generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, ChainGPT, and Gemini influence blockchain development. The research focuses on performance, security, and developer trust in AI-generated Solidity code.

  • Surveyed 114 developers to capture perceptions, usage patterns, and trust dynamics.
  • Evaluated AI-generated contracts for compilation success, unit testing, and static-analysis compliance.
  • Published in BSCI 2025 and forms the empirical component of dissertation Study 3.
