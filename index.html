About Me

I am an Assistant Professor of Instruction in the Division of Data Science at the University of Texas at Arlington. I earned my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Virginia Tech in August 2026, where I worked with Dr. Chris Brown on blockchain-oriented software engineering and AI-assisted security analysis.

My research examines how large language models (LLMs) and empirical methods can improve software security. I developed ChainSentinel, a multi-agent approach to smart contract auditing, and co-authored an ESEM 2026 study comparing seven static and LLM-based security tools across 316 Solidity contracts from two benchmarks. My work also investigates how developers build, test, and evaluate blockchain software.

At UTA, I teach Python and Machine Learning. I have also developed Applied Agentic AI and LLM Systems (DATA 4392 / ASDS 6300) for Spring 2027, a project-based course covering LLM applications, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), tool-using agents, and evaluation.

My research interests include:

  • AI for Software Engineering and Security: Applying LLMs and multi-agent workflows to vulnerability analysis.
  • Empirical Software Engineering: Evaluating tool performance and understanding developer practices.
  • Blockchain Software Engineering: Improving the reliability and security of smart contract development.
  • Human-AI Collaboration: Studying how to make AI-assisted analysis more useful and trustworthy.
